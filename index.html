About the project

An ontology of creativity across twenty years of fashion film

This project extends CreOn — an integrated OWL2 ontology of creativity theories — by applying it to a concrete cultural artifact: the two Devil Wears Prada films, separated by twenty years.

The core contribution is the modeling of opera aperta and fruition: how the same CreativeProduct acquires different meanings across different reception contexts, driven by changes in CreativityPress.

We introduce four new classes — FruitionContext, ReceptionShift, MediaIndustryPress, and SequelProcess — directly addressing open questions in the CreOn research agenda.

Rhodes' 4P Theory
Person · Process · Product · Press — the four dimensions of creativity mapped to the film's two productions
Csikszentmihalyi
Domain/Field model — fashion industry as social domain, Hollywood as field of validation
Opera Aperta
Same CreativeProduct, different meaning across FruitionContexts (2006 → #MeToo → 2026)
Corazza's Model
Mental/social process — same creative agents, same social domain, radically different press context
Requirements

Three stories, three dimensions of creativity

A
Story A · Person + Process

The Same Hands, Twenty Years Apart

David Frankel and Aline Brosh McKenna produced both films. In 2004, they worked within print-era Hollywood on a $35M budget. In 2024, the same agents returned to a $100M sequel in the streaming age, with the film itself explicitly addressing the death of print media. The PersonalProcess is structurally identical — but the CreativityPress transformed everything.

B
Story B · Product + Press

Cerulean as Cultural Memory

The 2006 film became a cultural phenomenon: it shaped fashion journalism's public image, made 'cerulean' a global reference, and turned Miranda Priestly into an archetype. When the sequel launched in 2026, audiences did not arrive with clean eyes — they brought twenty years of memes, reruns, and commentary. The original CreativeProduct's meaning had already shifted before the sequel existed.

C
Story C · Opera Aperta

Three Audiences, One Film

Audiences in 2006 read TDWP as a story about ambition and its cost. After #MeToo in 2017, the same film was reread through the lens of workplace abuse. In 2026, after the streaming sequel, the original became a nostalgic document of a vanished media world. The film did not change — its FruitionContexts did. This is opera aperta: the CreativeProduct is ontologically stable; its interpretive valence is not.

T-Box

New Classes & Properties

New Classes

:FruitionContext
rdfs:subClassOf creon:CreativitySituation
A situated context in which a CreativeProduct is received and interpreted by an audience, independent of the original production. Subclasses: TheatricalFruitionContext, StreamingFruitionContext, CriticalFruitionContext, SocialMediaFruitionContext.
:ReceptionShift
rdfs:subClassOf dul:Event
An event marking a change in dominant interpretive frame across two FruitionContexts, triggered by a CreativityPress change. Subclasses: SocialMovementShift, TechnologicalShift, HistoricalShift.
:MediaIndustryPress
rdfs:subClassOf creon:CreativityPress
Structural changes in the media/entertainment industry acting on PersonalProcess. Subclasses: PrintMediaPress, StreamingMediaPress, SocialMediaPress.
:CulturalPressEvent
rdfs:subClassOf creon:CreativityPress
A social or cultural event acting as CreativityPress on reception. Subclasses: SocialMovementPress, PoliticalPressEvent.
:SequelProcess
rdfs:subClassOf creon:PersonalProcess
A PersonalProcess whose agents revisit a prior PersonalProcess. Subclasses: DirectSequel, RevivalSequel.
:ConceptualInterpretiveFrame
rdfs:subClassOf creon:MetaConvergence
A conceptual frame organising interpretation of a CreativeProduct. Subclasses: FeministFrame, NostalgiaFrame, CareerNarrativeFrame, SatireFrame.
:FashionFilm
rdfs:subClassOf creon:CreativeProduct
A film whose primary creative domain is the fashion industry. Subclass: FashionFilmSequel.
:FilmRole · :AudienceType
rdfs:subClassOf dul:SocialObject · dul:CollectiveAgent
FilmRole subclasses: DirectorRole, ScreenwriterRole, ActorRole, ProducerRole. AudienceType subclasses: TheatricalAudience, StreamingAudience, CriticalAudience, CasualRewatchAudience.

New Object Properties

:hasReceptionContext
CreativeProduct → FruitionContext
Links a product to its reception contexts over time. Inverse: :interpretsFruitionOf.
:interpretedThrough
FruitionContext → ConceptualInterpretiveFrame
The conceptual frame applied in a given reception context.
:fromContext · :toContext
ReceptionShift → FruitionContext
Start and end FruitionContext of a ReceptionShift event.
:triggeredByPress
ReceptionShift → creon:CreativityPress
The press change that caused the reception shift.
:shiftsInterpretationOf
ReceptionShift → creon:CreativeProduct
The CreativeProduct whose interpretive frame shifts in this event.
:continuesFrom
SequelProcess → creon:PersonalProcess
Links a sequel process to its predecessor. Cardinality exactly 1.
:isSequelOf · :adaptedFrom
FashionFilmSequel → FashionFilm · FashionFilm → SourceNovel
Direct sequel and source-material links between creative products.
:hasSocialDomain · :hasAudienceAgent
CreativitySituation → SocialObject · FruitionContext → Agent
Social domain and audience agent links in creativity and reception contexts.

Datatype Properties

:hasReceptionYear · :hasReleaseYear
FruitionContext → xsd:gYear · CreativeProduct → xsd:gYear
Year of a given reception context or original product release.
:hasBudgetUSD · :hasBoxOfficeUSD
PersonalProcess → xsd:integer · FashionFilm → xsd:integer
Production budget and total theatrical box office gross in USD.
:hasDurationMinutes · :hasProductionYear
FashionFilm → xsd:integer · PersonalProcess → xsd:gYear
Film duration in minutes and year production began.
